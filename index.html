How a transfer moves

From posting to paid, without the back-and-forth.

01

Post

The plant lists the material with its waste classification, purity range, quantity and pickup site.

02

Match

The match engine checks every open buyer request and opens a deal room with each compatible buyer instantly.

03

Negotiate

Buyer and seller message, send offers and accept terms inside the deal room.

04

Comply and ship

WasteXchange prepares the required transfer documents and books a qualified carrier.

05

Settle

The seller is paid in full on delivery acceptance. The buyer pays the 7.5% exchange fee.

Match engine

What gets checked before two companies are connected.

Gates must pass or no match is made. Weighted factors rank the matches that remain on a 100 point score.

Gate

Material category

Seller's material and buyer's request are the same material family.

Gate

Legal classification

Non-hazardous, universal waste, used oil or RCRA hazardous must be a class the buyer is permitted to receive.

Gate

Usable spec range

The seller's purity or concentration range must overlap the buyer's acceptable range.

Up to 30 pts

Spec fit

How much of the seller's range sits inside what the buyer can use.

Up to 25 pts each

Quantity and distance

Volume against the buyer's receiving limit, and pickup site against the buyer's sourcing radius.

Up to 20 pts

Price fit

Asking price against the buyer's maximum, converted to the same unit. A gap is flagged for negotiation, never hidden.

Why it matters

Circular, local, and on the record.

  • Plants lower disposal costs by selling what they used to pay to haul away.
  • Local businesses cut input costs with material that meets their spec.
  • Every completed transfer carries documentation of diversion from landfill, ready for ESG reporting.
  • Short hauls between nearby companies keep material and money in the regional economy.
